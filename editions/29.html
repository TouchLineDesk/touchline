Season 3 · No. 2 · September

The month the table stopped making sense

September had 35 league games and 206 goals, the conference groups, and the first two rounds of the Youth League. Florida, the June preview’s sixteenth, are top. Michigan lost at North Carolina and are fifth, and the model still has them favourites. Texas Tech went to Austin and won the Caprock Lamp 4–0. Texas scored fifteen in the Youth League in Ostrava and have lost four league games. Miami are bottom of the Sixteen. The first real grades are in, the 2024 board has a new No. 2, and the Pan American Games are coming in October, so the desk has picked the twenty-three it would send.

How this edition was made. Every number is read from the save: the table and every league goal, each player’s minutes, ratings and season totals, abilities, hidden attributes, the Youth League and conference fixtures, and the pro clubs’ figures for the graduates. One change from August: the save now keeps a player’s season as one competitive total, league plus conference groups plus Youth League, so player totals in this edition are all competitions, friendlies excluded. League-only goals are counted from the league scoresheets and are labelled as such. The grades are the first of the season to use a real minutes bar, 360. The Pan American roster is the desk’s pick; the save has no squad for it.

Round by round

September 2 to September 23 · 35 league games · 206 goals

Four league rounds, one of them three games long because the conference groups took the midweeks. The Sixteen played the six clubs outside it sixteen times and won fifteen, by 104–8; the sixteenth was Akron’s 0–0 with North Carolina. Between themselves the Sixteen played eighteen games and drew three. In August that number was three as well, and all three were 4–4; this month the draws were 3–3, 3–3 and 1–1.

12–0Florida State v TCU, Sep 2

The biggest win of the season. Ozzie Cisneros scored at 2, 32 and 37 (a penalty), Nicholas Gioacchini at 12, 68 and 77. TCU had Logan Makrecky sent off at 20 with the score 3–0. Florida State then lost their next two league games.

0–4Texas v Texas Tech, Sep 23

The Caprock Lamp. Brayan Castellanos turned the ball into his own net at two minutes, and Omar Sowe and two from Giovanni Reyna did the rest. Texas Tech have held the lamp since December; Texas have now lost four league games this season, three of them in September.

4–3North Carolina v Michigan, Sep 2

Michigan’s first league defeat. Quinn Sullivan scored inside two minutes, Robert Korshunov twice either side of half-time, Arquimides Ordonez at 64. Michigan scored through Isaiah Parker, Damian Rivera and Brian Gutiérrez and conceded four, as many as in their other five league games put together.

4–3Washington v Florida State, Sep 23

Florida State led 3–1 at 55 minutes through Efrain Alvarez, Dean Price and Amet Korça. Mario Penagos scored his second at 58, and Juan Alvarez scored at 85 and 90. Washington are third; Florida State, top on August 31, are sixth.

Sat Sep 2 · Sun Sep 3Round five

11 games, 76 goals. Florida State 12–0 TCU and Texas 9–0 at Tulane, where George Johnson scored his first two league goals of the season. Washington won 7–2 at Clemson with three late goals from Sagir Arce. UCLA won 8–0 at Notre Dame, two from Paxten Aaronson just before half-time. Texas Tech beat Alabama 4–1. Oklahoma beat LSU 4–1, with LSU’s Ray Serrano sent off at 67. North Carolina beat Michigan 4–3. On the Sunday Ohio State won 4–1 in Miami.

Sat Sep 9A short round

Three league games around the conference groups. UCLA beat Texas 2–0. Florida won 3–2 at Oklahoma, all three of their goals inside 49 minutes; Jackson Conway’s penalty at 90 made it close. Michigan beat Akron 5–1.

Sat Sep 16 · Sun Sep 17Round seven

11 games, 62 goals. Georgia won 10–0 at Louisville: four from Jose Villaseñor before half-time, three from Richard Ledezma. USC won 9–0 at Clemson, three from Santiago Muñoz. Michigan won 6–1 at Rice with three from Sebastian Soto. Akron drew 0–0 with North Carolina, the first point any of the six has taken from the Sixteen this season. Yunus Musah scored twice in three minutes for Notre Dame against Texas, after George Johnson had put Texas ahead. Florida won 3–2 in Miami with a goal from Carlos Teran at 90. LSU won 3–2 at Florida State, two from Jesus Ferreira. Oklahoma beat Ohio State 5–3 despite a Ricardo Pepi hat-trick.

Sat Sep 23Round eight

10 games, 55 goals. Florida 10–1 Rice, three from Missael Rodriguez and three from Daniel Rios. Texas Tech won the Caprock Lamp 4–0 in Austin. Washington came from 1–3 down to beat Florida State 4–3. Georgia and Miami drew 1–1, Harrison Robledo and Jack Imperato, the storyline checkpoint for both. USC and Oklahoma drew 3–3, Muñoz equalising at 90 after Malik Tillman had scored twice. UCLA and North Carolina drew 3–3, Korshunov and Paxten Aaronson both scoring at 90. Notre Dame won 3–2 at Alabama with Malick Sanogo’s goal at 90. TCU beat Tulane 3–0 in the only game between two of the six.

Seventeen goals in 8 games. Five of them were in the league: three in the 9–0 at Clemson, one at Louisville, and the equaliser at 90 against Oklahoma that kept USC unbeaten. The other twelve came in the conference groups, where USC are 36–0 in five. His average rating for the month is 9.14, the best of anyone with 300 minutes. He has 21 goals for the season, which is the most in the Academy and puts him at 21 of the record 58, in October.

The case against him is the opposition, and it is a real case. The best of September’s goals were Musah’s two in three minutes against Texas, Ferreira’s two at Florida State, Juan Alvarez’s two in the last five minutes against Florida State. Muñoz’s were mostly against Clemson and the conference schools. The case for him is that the save rates every game, and on average he was the best player on the pitch across all 8 of them. He is 21, rated 133, a ceiling of 144, and his first season grade is 99.0.
