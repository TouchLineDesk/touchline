The December edition · Season 2 · No. 6

Five for five, and the view from the top

Michigan started December third and finished it first, with a perfect month — five league games, five wins — and the season’s first half closes with the reigning world club youth champions two points clear. Georgia, top and unbeaten on December 2, lost twice. Washington, top on October 31, took one point from twelve. Miami went from eleventh to fifth. And Notre Dame’s Yunus Musah had the month of anybody in the country.

December

December 3–31 · league games only

The month in one table: what each of the Sixteen did between the November edition and tonight. Michigan alone were perfect. Seven clubs took twelve points or more. Washington took one.

15/15 Michigan

A perfect month

Five league games, five wins, twenty goals of difference. Michigan were third on December 2 and are first on December 31; their December form line is the only five-for-five among the Sixteen besides USC’s four-from-four. They have 103 league goals, the most in the division, and their two strikers lead the league scoring race on league goals alone — Sebastian Soto 16, Folarin Balogun 15.

1/12 Washington

The fall

Top of the table on October 31. Fourth on December 2. Twelfth tonight, after a December of three defeats and a draw from four games. They have the fourth-highest top-eleven ability in the league (118.2) and the second-lowest top-eleven grade (64.2) — the widest gap between what Washington have and what they are doing that this desk can measure.

+26 Miami

The climb

Four wins and a draw, twenty-six goals of difference, and up six places to fifth. Miami have the most clean sheets in the league (10) and are one of two sides — with Michigan — unbeaten in nine. This afternoon: 6–0 at Louisville, two each for Daniel Steedman and Jordan Adebayo-Smith.

4/4 USC

The perfect quiet

Four games, four wins, and the only form line in the league reading WWWWW alongside Michigan’s. Up three places to tenth — the smallest headline in this issue and one of its better stories.

2–1–2 Georgia

The run ends

Georgia went sixteen league games unbeaten and finished November top. December brought their first two defeats, seven points from fifteen and minus one in goal difference. They still concede fewer than anybody (22 in 21) and still have the best player in Touchline Grades. They are fourth.

5 pts The six outside

Twenty-eight games, five points

Akron, TCU, Clemson, Louisville, Duke and Oregon played twenty-eight league games between them in December and took five points. Four of them have none. Oregon have gone eighteen league games without a win, Louisville sixteen, Duke thirteen.

Player of the month

December 3–31 · all competitions
Notre Dame · Winger · 20 years old

Yunus Musah

5Goals
6Assists
3Player of the match
8.71Avg rating*
203Minutes

Eleven goal involvements in 203 minutes — one every eighteen and a half. Three player-of-the-match awards in a month, and seven in the league this season, the most of anyone. He is eleven from eleven from the penalty spot in all competitions, the only regular taker in the league who has not missed.

Musah’s grade jumped from 78.1 to 85.7 in four weeks, the biggest rise among the league’s top players, and he is now the fourth-best winger in Touchline Grades. Notre Dame are fourteenth. He has done this for a team that took six points from twelve around him, which is the strongest case for him and the reason he will not be in our halftime XI’s first eleven yet.

The halftime awards

Touchline’s own · not the league’s · all competitions, Aug–Dec

The season is half over, the league hands out nothing until June, and we cannot wait that long. Nine standard awards, two All-Halftime elevens and a dozen we made up. Every figure is read from the save; the judgment is ours.

Most Valuable Player
Milan Iloski LSU · 22 · Advanced Forward37 goals · 8 assists · grade 95.6 · 1,759 min

Thirty-seven goals in all competitions, twelve more than his expected goals said he would score — the most clinical season in the league by a distance — and still the Youth League’s top scorer from the autumn. LSU are thirteenth; without him they would be lower. The grade agrees that he is a top-two player in the country, not just its leading scorer.

Runners-up: Juan Pablo Torres (UGA), Matko Miljevic (MICH)

Playmaker of the half
Juan Pablo Torres Georgia · 22 · Winger28 assists · 198 key passes · grade 96.7

Twenty-eight assists, five more than anybody else, 198 key passes and the No. 1 grade in the league. Torres finished November at the ceiling of the scale on 99.0 and has come down only because December was ordinary for everyone at Georgia.

Runners-up: Miguel Ramírez (MIA, 23 assists), Matko Miljevic (MICH, 22)

Golden Glove
Gabriel Słonina Alabama · 18 · Sweeper Keeper8 clean sheets · 4 conceded · 92% saves vs 89% expected

Four goals conceded in 990 minutes, eight clean sheets, and three percentage points of save rate above what an average keeper would have managed with the same shots. The minutes are low — he has missed time — and that is the only argument against him.

Runners-up: Jeff Dewsnup (UNC, 13 clean sheets, 1,710 min), Brady Scott (MIA)

Defender of the half
Josh O'Brien Miami · 19 · Ball-Playing Defender13 goals · 92 interceptions · 363 headers · grade 90.1

A nineteen-year-old centre-back with thirteen goals. O’Brien also has the second-most interceptions in the league (92) and the second-most headers (363); he is the only defender above 90 in Touchline Grades and the spine of the defence with the most clean sheets.

Runners-up: Michael Edwards (TTU), Owen Otasowie (TTU)

Wing back of the half
Taylor Booth Texas Tech · 21 · Inverted Wing Back10 goals · 16 assists · 49 chances created · grade 92.2

The best inverted wing back in the league by seven grade points. Booth creates more clear-cut chances than anyone at any position (49) while playing a defensive role on paper.

Runners-up: Marcos López (OSU), Bret Halsey (MICH)

Young Player (19 and under)
Evan Rotundo Oklahoma · 18 · Shadow Striker8.22 average · 11 goals · 13 assists · now Gold

The best average rating of any regular in the league at eighteen years old, a promotion to the Gold tier in the autumn and twenty-four goal involvements for the team third in the table. Słonina and Jonathan Perez grade higher; Rotundo has done it in a better team’s attack.

Runners-up: Gabriel Słonina (BAMA, 18), Jonathan Perez (MIA, 19), Josh O’Brien (MIA, 19)

Freshman of the half (17 and under)
George Johnson Texas · 16 · Advanced Forward19 goals at 16 · CA 113 · potential 180

Nineteen goals at sixteen. Johnson is the only player in the league with a Ruby ceiling — his potential of 180 is the highest of all 352 — and he put on three more ability points in December. Brooklyn Raines (LSU, 17) grades higher as a holding midfielder; nobody his age has done what Johnson has done in the box.

Runners-up: Brooklyn Raines (LSU, 17), Oli Griffin (LSU, 16)

Runners-up: Craig Brown (ND, +20), José Roberto Rosales (UGA, +20), Matt Spear (UCLA, +19)

The Garrett file, No. 5

Rumblings · a recurring item

A national title, and a winter in Ann Arbor

Two things happened in December, and only one of them is a coincidence.

The first: Livonia Metro’s Under-14s — Daniel Garrett’s own age group, the side he plays for when he is not playing up with the Under-15s we have been streaming — won the national title in their division. We did not stream it and we will not pretend we watched it. What we can say is that the trophy is real, the side is his, and he is fourteen years old with a national championship.

The second: the outdoor season ended, and Garrett has spent the winter playing indoor soccer. The facility is in Ann Arbor.

That is the same city as the main stand he sat in on November 12, and the same city as the club that is now top of the Academy table. There is an obvious explanation that has nothing to do with Michigan: Ann Arbor is half an hour from Livonia, and good indoor football in a Michigan winter is not easy to find. We are printing the less obvious one only as a question.

“It’s close and the surface is good. That’s why we go.” — his father, asked by Jess Ocampo outside the facility, before the question had been finished

Daniel himself said nothing about Michigan. Asked about the title, he said: “We were the better team in the final. I was more tired in the semi.” Asked whether anybody from a college programme had been in to watch him indoors, he looked at his father again.
