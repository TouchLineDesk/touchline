New Recruits · Signing Season

Special section · the March intake, March 14 to 31

Every spring the Sixteen restock. Between the middle of March and the second week of April the schools take in their new players: the recruits, who arrive with no record at another named club this season and are 18 or younger, most of them fifteen or sixteen; and the walk-ons, who come with a record somewhere else (another college programme, a lower-league club or a professional one) or are older. Touchline had no file on any of them until today.

The count so far is 51: 29 recruits and 22 walk-ons, at every one of the sixteen schools. Notre Dame took six, Michigan and Ohio State five each; USC and Texas Tech one each. Rated on Touchline’s recruiting scale (stars by potential ability), the class has 4 five-star players, 5 four-stars, 31 three-stars and 11 two-stars.

Three of them have a higher ceiling than anyone already in the league except George Johnson, and one has a higher ceiling than his. Daniel Garrett signed for Michigan in January and arrived with this class. He has already played a game. He is the only Diamond card in the league.

The next great player?

Potential is a ceiling, not a forecast. George Johnson arrived last year with a ceiling of 180 and has gone from 98 to 119 in a year; he is still 61 points short of it. With that in mind, three of these players could become something the league has not had:

  • Daniel Garrett starts higher than anyone ever has here. At 125 he is already a Gold card; if he grows as fast as Johnson did, he passes 150 before he is seventeen. The save has given him the professionalism to do it.
  • Lance Thompson is the safest bet: 110 already, the best technique in the class, and seven years before his draft. A Miami side with him in the middle and Ramírez and Perez outside him could be the best midfield in the league.
  • Dan Dickinson has the highest ceiling of anyone without a Diamond card apart from George Johnson, at 178. He is the furthest from it of the five-stars, and his big-game numbers are the lowest. He is the gamble.

Of the five-stars, only Garrett has played. The rest will be followed in The Next Ones from April.

March

February 25 (evening) to March 29 · league games only

Only two of the Sixteen went through the month unbeaten, Alabama and Notre Dame, and Alabama took the most points, 13 from 15. Georgia, Texas and Notre Dame took 12 each. Of the two games between the top three, Texas won in Athens and Georgia beat Michigan at home; four days after winning in Athens, Texas lost at Alabama. At the bottom, Clemson and Oregon lost all five.

13 Alabama · points

The best month

Five games, no defeats: 4–0 at Clemson, 7–0 against Duke, 4–2 against Texas with a Jack Lynn hat-trick, 3–3 at Washington and 9–3 at Louisville. Up two places to fifth, and their chance of a top-five finish has gone from 17% to 37%.

4–2 Georgia

Beaten at home, then level

Texas won 2–1 in Athens on March 11, Georgia’s only home defeat of the season. Four days later they beat Michigan 4–2: Sergiño Dest, Will Sands, Alex Mighten and Benjamin Ofeimu. Then 10–0 at Louisville, with hat-tricks for Richard Ledezma and Jose Villaseñor, and 3–2 at USC, Ledezma in the 92nd minute.

3–3 Michigan

Top on goal difference

The month began with a 3–3 against USC in which Michigan scored in the 88th and 94th minutes to lead and conceded in the 95th. Folarin Balogun scored four at Oregon and Tyler Freeman four against Akron, but the 4–2 in Athens cost them their two-point cushion. Sebastian Soto’s hat-trick beat Oklahoma 7–3 on Wednesday.

2–1 Texas

One point behind

Josh Sargent and Jonathan Gómez won it in Athens. Gómez had scored four in the 9–0 against Akron a week before. Texas then lost 4–2 at Alabama, beat Oklahoma 4–3 with two from Ariel Mbumba and Florida 5–2. Twelve points, the same as Georgia.

2.1% Oklahoma

Out of it, probably

Three wins by an aggregate of 18–2, then 4–3 at Texas and 7–3 at Michigan. Oklahoma are fourth, four points off the top, and their title chance has gone from 9.5% to 2.1%.

3–4 Miami

The shield goes north

Miami scored 18 in three wins over TCU, Texas Tech and North Carolina and lost the other two: 2–0 at Notre Dame and 4–3 at home to Florida State, which hands the Peninsula Shield to Tallahassee. Jordan Adebayo-Smith scored seven league goals, four of them against TCU.
