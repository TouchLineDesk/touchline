Special edition · the 2022 World Cup

Fourteen of the twenty-three

The United States go to Qatar having won nine of ten games this year, with the thirteenth-best ranking in the world and a squad built in this league: eleven current Academy players and the first three picks of last summer’s draft. They also go with Erling Haaland and Kevin De Bruyne in their group.

By the Touchline desk · read from the save on Sunday, November 20, 2022, the day before kick-off

What this edition is. A World Cup issue and nothing else. The twenty-three and where each of them comes from; Touchline’s starting eleven in the shape this program plays — a sweeper keeper, two ball-playing centre-backs, inverted wing-backs, one holding midfielder, a line of three behind two strikers; a match-by-match forecast of Group F; how far the U.S.A. should go and what has to happen for them to go further; all thirty-two squads rated; the favourites, the dark horses and the players worth the ticket; and our bracket, all the way to December 18.

How the numbers work. Every squad was read from the save today. A team’s strength is the average ability of its best eleven, nudged by its world-ranking points. We then played the tournament twenty thousand times. The percentages in this edition are those twenty thousand tournaments, not a feeling. They are also only as good as ability ratings, which is to say: useful, and wrong often enough to make the thing worth watching.

Goal. Zack Steffen. He has played eight of the ten internationals this year at a 7.20 average; Matt Turner has played one, at 6.70. Steffen is also the higher-rated of the two (138 to 132). A side that pushes its wing-backs into midfield needs a keeper who will come to the edge of his box.

Centre-backs. Carter-Vickers and Brooks: the two best centre-backs in the squad by our ball-playing-defender score, and the two biggest. Strength 17 and 16, jumping 13 and 17. That is not an accident with Haaland on the fixture list. Chris Richards is the first defender off the bench and the only one who can also play right-sided.

Inverted wing-backs. This is the call we like most. Tyler Adams was drafted third as a holding midfielder, and he is listed at right wing-back too. Playing him there means that when the U.S.A. have the ball there are two holding midfielders — Adams stepping in from the right beside McKennie — and when they lose it there is a natural full-back already in position. Dest takes the left, where Georgia have played him into ten goals this season. George Bello is the like-for-like replacement.

Holding midfielder. Weston McKennie, the second pick in June and the second-best player in the squad. He has the best role fit in the whole squad for inverted wing-back as well, which is why he goes in the middle, where everything passes through.

The three. Pulisic on the left, where the winger score is highest for anyone in the squad (15.2). Giovanni Reyna in the hole behind the strikers: nine goals in ten internationals this year, the most of any American, at a 7.85 average. On the right, Konrad De la Fuente over Jack Harrison — close on the numbers, but De la Fuente is 21 with a ceiling of 162 and nine goals and nine assists at Washington.

Two strikers. Folarin Balogun, 23 goals for Michigan and the best advanced-forward score in the squad, with Josh Sargent: 14 goals and 15 assists for Texas, five for his country this year. Sargent drops in and creates; Balogun runs beyond. Weah (22 goals at Ohio State) and Dike are the changes.

How far should they go?

The path, and the one game that decides it

Touchline’s call in October was second in the group, out in the round of 16. Having read all thirty-two squads, we are keeping it — and we are more sure of the first half than the second.

For comparison: Germany reach the quarter-finals in 80% of tournaments, Belgium in 52%, Norway in 8%.

Why the round of 16 is a wall

Group F is paired with Group E, and Group E is Spain and the Netherlands. Finish second and the U.S.A. play the winner of E — Spain in 58% of our tournaments, the Netherlands in 41%. Win the group and they play E’s runner-up, which is almost always the other one. There is no soft landing. Against Spain our model gives the U.S.A. a 12% chance of winning inside ninety minutes and about one in five of getting through once extra time and penalties are counted.

The quarter-final, if they get there, is probably Germany — the tournament favourites in our model.

The bad version · about two in five
Group stage

Haaland happens

Lose to Norway and the U.S.A. go through in only 13% of our tournaments. The group is closer than the rankings say: we have the U.S.A. and Norway almost level in strength, and one of them goes home with four points.

Group: 3rd

What the head coach should do

  1. Treat Egypt as a goal-difference game. Four points is the likely total for whoever finishes second, and goals are the tiebreak. Win by three or more and the U.S.A. go through in 67% of our tournaments; win by one and it is 58%.
  2. Play for the draw against Norway only after the hour. A win is worth 92% qualification and a draw 55%. Start to win it, and protect a point late: Busio for Reyna, Richards for a tiring wing-back.
  3. Look at the table before picking the Belgium team. If a draw is enough, start Parks next to McKennie and ask De Bruyne to find a way through two holding midfielders. If a win is needed, the eleven above is the one to do it with.
  4. Keep Adams at wing-back in the knockouts. Against Spain the U.S.A. will spend long spells without the ball. A wing-back who is really a holding midfielder is the difference between a back four and a back five when they need one.

A note on the bracket

The round-of-16 pairings follow the standard World Cup bracket, where each group winner plays a runner-up from its partner group (A with B, C with D, E with F, G with H) and the two halves meet only in the final. Our model picks the team with the higher strength in every tie. Real tournaments do not work like that, which is why the chance that all of these picks come true is well under one in a thousand.

Touchline

Touchline is fiction. It’s an in-universe sports desk for a Football Manager 2022 save. Every squad, ability figure, attribute, appearance, goal and rating on this page was read from the save on November 20, 2022, the day before the tournament. Head coaches are referred to by role only. Real players appear only through their record in the save; no real player is quoted.

The model is this desk’s own. Team strength is the average current ability of each squad’s best eleven (best goalkeeper plus the ten best outfield players), adjusted by world-ranking points. Matches are simulated as independent Poisson scorelines; knockout ties add extra time and penalties. Twenty thousand tournaments. Role fit is the average of each role’s key attributes, read from the save. None of these are figures the game produces.

All quotes are invented for this fictional universe. They come only from Touchline’s fictional panel. Flags are from the open-source flag-icons set.
