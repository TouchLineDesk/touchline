Season 3 preview

Same two clubs, different order

Season 3 kicks off on August 19, and the champions open at North Carolina. Georgia are favourites in 20,000 simulated seasons. Michigan top the desk’s power rankings for the second summer running. Last year the rankings had Michigan first and Georgia second, and Georgia won.

The Touchline desk · read from the save on June 30, 2023

84graduates gone to the pros on June 20, with 9,310 points of ability
13of the U.S. Gold Cup 23 have played in the Sixteen
75%the title odds of Georgia, Michigan and Miami combined
462league games, Aug 19 to Jun 1, every one already on the fixture list

Nobody in the Sixteen signed anyone this summer. The draft took 84 players and every club now carries exactly 22. That makes this preview simple to build and hard to call. Every number below comes from the save: each squad’s current ability, last season’s results and the real Season 3 fixture list. The 84 departures are the main reason the order has changed.

The simulations use last season’s attack and defence for each club, adjusted for what the draft took away and shrunk 20% towards the average. They make Georgia favourites at 34%, Michigan second at 21% and Miami a close third at 20%, despite Miami losing Jonathan David and Brady Scott. The power rankings weigh the starting eleven, the bench and potential more heavily and come out differently. Both are below. Texas Tech lost two players and have the best projected eleven in the league.

The Gold Cup

Group C · after matchday one · June 28

The United States opened their Gold Cup on June 28 with a 3–0 win over Trinidad & Tobago in front of 92,542. Konrad De la Fuente of Washington scored twice. Daryl Dike, Cincinnati’s No. 4 pick eight days earlier, scored the other. Nine of the starting eleven have played in the Sixteen.

The scoreline flatters nobody. The United States had 26 shots to five and could have scored more. Trinidad & Tobago were already a goal down when George went off, and the second and third goals came inside eight minutes after that. All three goals and all three assists came from Academy players: a current Washington winger, a Cincinnati rookie, a Georgia full-back and Weston McKennie, the No. 2 pick of 2022.

Thirteen of the twenty-three. Seven are still at a school in the Sixteen, three were drafted in 2022 and three were drafted eight days before the U.S. opener. The U.S. senior squad has one Academy player fewer than the fourteen at the World Cup in November, and the Academy players are now the starters: nine of the eleven on Wednesday. Bryan Okoh and Ricardo Pepi won the U20 World Cup eleven days before the opener, and Okoh started at centre-back and rated 8.1.

What it costs the Sixteen. Seven current players are in the squad: De la Fuente and Busio (Washington), Reyna (Texas Tech), Dest (Georgia), Musah (Notre Dame), Pepi (Ohio State) and Okoh (Texas). Group C ends on July 6. A run to the final would take them into mid-July and past the start of most pre-season training. The pre-season tournaments start on July 28 and the league on August 19. Washington are the club most exposed, with two of their best three players in the squad.

Draft 23 · 84 players · by share of last season’s minutes

Power rankings

Pre-season · best to worst

The desk’s order is a blend: half the projected points from the simulations, 30% the starting eleven’s ability, 10% the bench and 10% potential. Michigan come out top because they have the deepest squad and the highest ceiling. Georgia are second because the model rates them highest. Under each rank is where the desk had the club last August, for reference. Last August’s top three were Michigan, Georgia and Texas Tech; they finished second, first and eighth.

Two in three it’s not Georgia. A 34% favourite is less likely to win than the field. Last August the desk ranked Michigan first, and they finished second, three points behind Georgia.

The top five is close to settled. Georgia, Michigan and Miami make it in more than three runs in four. The last two places are an open race between Texas, Texas Tech, Florida State, Oklahoma and North Carolina, all projected within four points of each other.

Relegation is a two-from-four. Clemson (74%) and TCU (63%) are the most likely to go down. Tulane and Rice come up with stronger squads than Duke and Oregon, the two clubs they replace. The non-Sixteen squads are still being built (TCU have eight players registered), so these numbers will move.

Model: Poisson goals, home advantage fitted on Season 2. Draft effect: about +0.03 attack and +0.04 defence per point of top-14 average ability, fitted across the Sixteen. Ties in the table are broken on goal difference, then goals scored.

Ten things to watch

Season 3 storylines
  • 1. Georgia without the treble’s spineThe champions open at North Carolina on August 19, a rematch of May’s Youth League final, which Georgia won 3–1. The players who led the treble are gone: Torres, Damus and Pellegrini scored or set up 185 goals between them. Can Dest, Henry and Villaseñor carry it? Georgia are the favourites to win the title but less likely to win it than not.
  • 2. Michigan’s goalkeeper, againA year ago the desk said Michigan had the best squad apart from a goalkeeper, and they finished second by three points. Antonio Carrera is 99 now, up from 91. Michigan again have the deepest bench and the highest ceiling, and again the weakest first-choice goalkeeper of the contenders.
  • 3. A record chase with no record-holdersDamus and Iloski, who share the goals record of 58, have both gone to the pros. Balogun, who held the record at 50 before them, is the best returning scorer with 44. Shashoua’s 51 assists from Season 1 is the record Matko Miljevic (40) can realistically break.
  • 4. The Diamond and the Ruby questionDaniel Garrett is fifteen, rated 129, potential 200. George Johnson turned seventeen on June 25 and is rated 122, potential 180. Nobody in the Academy has yet reached Ruby, which is 180. Neither will get there this season, but both are likely to be among the ten best players in the league by May.
  • 5. Texas Tech’s last chance with this squadTwo players lost and the best starting eleven, but also the oldest squad, with nine players eligible for next June’s draft. If this group is going to win the league, it has to be this season. Last summer the desk had them third and they finished eighth.
  • 6. Miami against the modelThe simulations rate Miami third. On ability, the eleven is only eleventh-best. One of the two is wrong. Miami lost 43% of their goals, plus Brady Scott’s 19 clean sheets, and the new goalkeeper is seventeen.
  • 7. The Gold Cup sevenDe la Fuente, Busio, Reyna, Dest, Musah, Pepi and Okoh are with the national team in July. The pre-season tournaments start on July 28. Washington, with two of the seven, are the club most exposed if the U.S.A. go deep.
  • 8. Ohio State’s sixteen-year-oldTyler Whitesides is rated 74 and is Ohio State’s best remaining goalkeeper. Both of last season’s goalkeepers were drafted. The best goalkeepers at the other fifteen schools are rated between 91 and 123.
  • 9. North Carolina, out of the Youth LeagueNorth Carolina lost the last two Youth League finals, both 3–1. This year they are not in it: seventh place missed the top five by six points.
  • 10. Three new opponents, one of them short-handedTulane and Rice replace Duke and Oregon. TCU, who finished 18th last season, have eight players on the books at the end of June. The Tobacco Road Milestone has no fixture this season, with Duke relegated.
