Season 2 · the preview

The holders aren’t in it

Michigan won the FIFA Youth League in Italy in May, finished sixth in their own league, four points short of the five places that qualify, and therefore have no place in the competition they are defending. They also, by our reading of every file in the Sixteen, now have the best squad in America. Season 2 starts on Saturday and almost nothing about it looks like Season 1.

Where this comes from. On August 10 the sixteen Academy squads hold 352 players between them — twenty-two each, exactly. Every ability and potential figure below is read from the save on that date; every goal, assist and rating is from last season, because this one has not started. The power ranking is a blend: half the projected starting eleven’s current ability, a fifth its potential, fifteen per cent bench strength (players twelve to eighteen) and fifteen per cent returning minutes, which is our proxy for how much of last season a club still has in the building. It is a projection made before a ball is kicked, and the whole point of publishing it now is that it can be wrong in public.

What the draft actually did

Sixteen squads, one transfer

Sixty-seven graduates left the Sixteen in Dallas on June 20. One player arrived: Ethan Wady, a twenty-year-old goalkeeper, to Michigan, the only signing any of these clubs made all summer. What is left is eerily tidy — every single club now carries exactly twenty-two players, because the draft took precisely the surplus each of them was holding and no more. Michigan were one short and went and bought one. Nobody else moved.

The competition itself has changed at both ends. Tennessee and Michigan State — who between them won two league matches all season and conceded more than three hundred goals — are gone. Up come the Akron Zips and the Louisville Cardinals, and it should be said plainly that we know nothing about either of them. They are not part of the Sixteen, so they are not in Touchline’s data, and the first honest assessment we can give of them will come after they have played. The other four outside clubs — TCU, Clemson, Oregon and Duke — finished seventeenth, eighteenth, nineteenth and twentieth of the twenty-two last season, and the gap between them and the Sixteen has not obviously narrowed.

The development desk

Eleven weeks of summer, and nobody got worse

Between the last day of Season 1 and this morning, 121 of the 351 returning players improved and not one regressed — an off-season, in this competition, is eleven weeks of free development and no games to lose it in. The gains are heavily weighted to the very young: the biggest riser in the league is a sixteen-year-old full-back at LSU who went up thirteen points without playing a competitive minute.

The Youth League, without its holders

Group-stage draw · Friday August 26

Five American places, decided by last season’s top five: UCLA, North Carolina, Georgia, Miami and LSU. The group-stage draw is on Friday August 26. Michigan, who beat North Carolina 3–1 in Viareggio on May 15 to win the thing for a second year running, finished sixth in the league — four points short of fifth — and are not in it. On our board they are now the strongest squad in the country. Three of the five who did qualify — UCLA, LSU and North Carolina — project in the bottom six.

The panel

On a league that turned itself upside down in one evening
DR
Dale Rourke
Rourke Unfiltered
UCLA won this league by fourteen points with the eleventh-best squad on paper and a clipping on the coach’s desk, and now every clever person in America has them fifteenth. I have read the numbers. The numbers are correct. I am still not doing this again. Whatever that building is running on, it did not graduate in June.
On the champions
NO
Nadia Okafor
The Whiteboard
Everybody is looking at the top of these squads. Look at the bottom. Every club in this competition now has exactly twenty-two players and forty-two matches plus, for five of them, a European trip. UCLA’s twelfth-to-eighteenth best players average eighty-seven. Alabama’s average eighty-nine. Two injuries in the same position and those are not squads any more, they are a starting eleven and a prayer.
On the twenty-two-man squads
TB
Theo Brandt
The Ledger
Six of the eight clubs that finished in the top half last season fall on this board, and seven of the eight that finished in the bottom half rise. The correlation between last season’s table and this projection is almost exactly zero. That has never been true of any league I have counted, and it has one cause: the draft takes twenty-three-year-olds, and good teams had more of them.
On the inversion
WK
Walt Kearney
Kearney’s Corner
Two clubs come up and this desk has published eleven thousand words about the other twenty without once mentioning either of them. That is not analysis, it is habit. Akron and Louisville have earned their way into a competition where sixteen clubs are covered and six are scenery, and I would like the first honest thing anybody writes about them to be written before they beat somebody.
On Akron and Louisville
HB
Hal Brannigan
The Brannigan Board
Eighty-two graduates. Sixty-seven picks last year. I have spent one summer telling people that nobody in this competition goes undrafted and I am now going to spend a year telling them the opposite. Jonathan David is the first name on the board and it is August. Circle it anyway — the interesting part is the fifteen players below him who will have moved forty points by May.
On next June

One caution before Saturday. Everything above is built on the 352 players who are in these buildings this morning, and there is a freshman intake still to come that none of these numbers can see. Last season the intake produced Joey Wallace, who went up six points in a fortnight, and a fifteen-year-old at Texas with a hundred and eighty of potential. The board that matters in May will have names on it that do not exist in this edition.
