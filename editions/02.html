USL Academy · Matchday 41 · Sunday

Win or bust, for both of them

Alabama must beat Tennessee on Sunday to keep their Youth League hopes alive. Tennessee must avoid defeat to stay in the league. At least one of them leaves with nothing.

Touchline Academy Desk

Saturday redrew the race for the top five. Georgia's 7–0 win at Clemson took them to 84 points, out of Alabama's reach, and sealed a Youth League place. Michigan and USC both won, but on 78 with one game left they can't catch Miami and LSU on 82, so both are out. That leaves one chaser. Alabama have 77 points and two games to play. Two wins would take them to 83, and then Miami or LSU would have to lose on the final day.

Tennessee's arithmetic is even harsher. Duke won 2–1 at Oregon on Saturday and moved to 14 points. Tennessee have 10 with two to play. If they lose in Alabama, they can finish on 13 at most, and they're down. A draw keeps them alive going into the last day at winless Michigan State.

History favours the hosts. Alabama won 7–0 in Tennessee in December and 4–0 in the Cotton Bowl group stage in January. Alabama are unbeaten in five league games, and their board's target is a top-five finish. Tennessee have won three league games all season.

“I've done the maths. We win twice and we need help. So we win twice, and then we find out.”

Alabama head coach

“Somebody leaves with nothing on Sunday. That's the best kind of football.”

Matchday 41, May 7

Georgia at Clemson
Clinched

González called it

Three days after telling Touchline that Georgia were "going back to the world stage," Steve González scored a hat-trick (26', 59', 67' pen) in a 7–0 win at Clemson. Georgia's 84 points put them out of Alabama's reach, so they are going back.

1–7
Miami at home to USC
Massacre

Seven at Miami, and still not enough

Brenden Aaronson scored in the 8th, 71st and 90th minutes as USC ended Miami's five-game winning run in the most brutal way possible. It changes nothing for USC: on 78 points with one game left, fifth place is out of reach.

2–1
Oklahoma over North Carolina
Warning shot

UNC stumble eight days before the final

Terran Campbell and Malik Tillman scored before half-time, and Arquimides Ordonez's 64th-minute goal wasn't enough for North Carolina. Next up for the Tar Heels are the Fiesta Bowl semi-final on Wednesday and Italy on the 15th.

3–0

Champions

The champions come off the gas

Owen Otasowie, Alexis Méndez from the spot and Giovanni Reyna scored. Vice-captain Ivy Brisma was sent off in the 49th minute. That makes two straight league defeats for UCLA.

The race for the world

UCLA, North Carolina and Georgia are through. Solid bars show current points, and the hatched area shows the most each club can still reach. The dashed red line is Miami and LSU's 82.

Alabama's route: beat Tennessee on Sunday, beat USC on May 28, and hope Miami (at home to Florida State) or LSU (at Washington) lose that day. Feuds, by the numbers

Rivalries written by results, not tradition
UNC 2 wins · UCLA 0 · 1 draw, UNC through on penalties

The champions' kryptonite

UCLA lost only five league games to the Sixteen before Saturday, and two of them were against North Carolina (4–3, 3–1). Then the Tar Heels ended UCLA's Youth League run on penalties after a 1–1 draw.

Florida 3 · Alabama 0 · aggregate 9–2

Gainesville owns Alabama

Florida won 4–1 in the league, 4–1 in SEC play and 1–0 in February. The two could meet again in the Cotton Bowl, which has an all-SEC final four.

Florida 2 · Georgia 2 · 1 draw · goals 10–12

The most even fight in the league

They have met five times: two preseason wins for Georgia, a 2–2 draw, a 3–2 Florida win in the league, and a 3–2 extra-time win for Florida in the Youth League quarter-final. González called it luck. Both are in the Cotton Bowl semis.

UCLA 2 · USC 0 · goals 8–1

Los Angeles belongs to UCLA

UCLA won 2–0 at home and 6–1 at USC. USC have just put seven past Miami, but they still haven't solved their neighbours.

Texas 3 · Oklahoma 0

Red River, one-way traffic

Texas won 4–2 and 3–1 in the league and 3–0 in Big 12 play. Texas have now lost four of their last five league games, including Saturday's 5–1 at home to Ohio State. Against Oklahoma, though, their record this season is perfect.
