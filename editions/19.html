Two points

Georgia started February fourth and finish it second, unbeaten, with 25 goals in four league games and one conceded since a 4–4 at Miami. Michigan are still top, but they lost 4–3 at home to Texas to a 94th-minute goal and drew 3–3 with LSU, and their lead is two points with twelve games left. Texas lost at home to USC this afternoon. Oklahoma lost 4–1 at Ohio State and then won three. Milan Iloski now has 51 goals this season in all competitions, one more than the Season 1 record. And on March 11 and March 15, Georgia play Texas and then Michigan, at home.

February

January 31 (evening) to February 25 · league games only

Only one of the Sixteen won every league game it played in February, and it was Texas Tech, with three. Georgia took ten points from twelve; Michigan took ten from fifteen, including the game in hand from January. The top four are separated by three points, and three of the games between them come in eight days in March.

4th→2nd Georgia

Unbeaten, and second

A 4–4 at Miami on February 4, then 3–1 against UCLA, 10–0 against Duke and 8–0 at Clemson. Ten points from twelve and a goal difference of +20 for the month. Georgia have conceded 33 league goals all season, the fewest in the division.

3–4 Texas

The win, and the slip

Six against Louisville, eight at Oregon, and on February 11 the result of the month: 4–3 at Michigan, Federico Oliva in the 94th minute. Then today, 3–2 at home to USC. Nine points, and third on goal difference ahead of Oklahoma.

10 pts Michigan

Still top, by less

The game in hand at the start of the month went 4–0 against Duke, then 2–0 at Washington and 7–0 at Louisville. In between, the 4–3 home defeat by Texas and a 3–3 with LSU in which Michigan led twice. The lead is two points, not five.

4–1 Oklahoma

One bad afternoon

Beaten 4–1 at Ohio State on February 4, where Timothy Weah scored three and Alan Robles was sent off. Then three wins, including 4–1 against Miami and 8–0 against Clemson. Level on 67 with Texas.

1 pt Florida

The month without a win

Lost 3–1 at Texas Tech, drew 2–2 with North Carolina, lost 5–0 at home to Miami and 3–0 at Ohio State. One point and minus ten. Sixteenth among the Sixteen, two points behind UCLA.

3 pts Florida State

Eight, then nothing

An 8–0 against TCU on the first Saturday, then a 5–1 defeat at Notre Dame and a 4–3 home defeat by Texas Tech after leading twice. Florida State play LSU tonight.

The run-in

New this month · the real fixture list, simulated 20,000 times

Touchline’s title odds used to play out an imaginary schedule. This month they play out the real one: the save holds every remaining fixture, with its date and kick-off, so each of the 20,000 simulated seasons plays the games that are actually left, in order. Team strength comes from goals for and against per game, pulled 20% toward the league average.

The biggest change is Georgia, from 10.1% to 30.4%. Some of that is February, and some is the calendar: Georgia play Texas and Michigan at home in the space of four days in March. Michigan are still favourites at 45.8%, down from 47.9%. Miami fall the furthest, from 16.0% to 3.6%, after the defeat at Oklahoma and the draw with Georgia.

Michigan, Georgia, Texas and Oklahoma have 12 league games left each. Texas Tech have played one game fewer than the clubs above them, and so have Florida State, LSU and Notre Dame.

Brilliant but brittle, confirmed

In the autumn this desk called Alabama “brilliant but brittle”, on the evidence of results. The save agrees, more bluntly than we did: Alabama’s players have the lowest average for important matches in the league, 7.7, more than half a point below the next club (Notre Dame, 8.4). They also have the lowest temperament. Alabama lost at home to UCLA and in stoppage time at Miami this month.

The record-breaker

Milan Iloski, who now has more goals in a season than anyone in the league’s history, has a consistency of 20, as high as the scale goes. His rating for important matches is 1, the bottom of the scale. He scores every week; whether he scores in a final is another question, and LSU are not in one yet.

Hidden attributes are the game’s own numbers, read from the save, and they can change as players age; Touchline will re-read them every month. Club figures are simple averages. They describe tendencies, not destinies: a squad of good big-game players still has to be good.
