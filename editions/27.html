Special edition · Season 3 · the storylines desk

Sixteen to follow.

In June the preview gave you ten things to watch. This is the standing version: sixteen storylines, each with a question, a number read from the save, the dates that decide it and a member of the panel whose name is on it. Every edition from August to June will carry an update on all sixteen. Nothing gets added mid-season. Nothing gets quietly dropped.

By the Touchline desk · abilities, fixtures and results read from the save on Monday, July 31, 2023, the first day of the last pre-season week

What makes the list. A storyline has to be something the game will actually answer. A title race answers itself. So does a sixteen-year-old’s ability number, a record chase, a draft board, a count of national-team call-ups. What does not make the list is anything the desk would have to invent an ending for. Every one of the sixteen has a line it rises on, a line it fades on, and a date it closes.

What the panel owes you. Each storyline belongs to one of six people. They wrote the opening line below and they will write the update every month. If a storyline dies, they say so and it is marked closed. If one turns into something else, that is the update. The tracker at the bottom of this page is the form every future edition will fill in.

Since the preview. The United States won the Gold Cup on July 16, 2–0 against Canada, scoring 31 goals in six games. Thirteen of the 23 came through the Sixteen and they scored 27 of the goals. The first pre-season tournament started on Friday and the second starts on Friday week. Season 3 opens on Saturday, August 19. The numbers in this edition are as of this morning.

How it works

The rules the desk is binding itself to

One question, one number

Every storyline opens with a question the game will answer and a baseline read from the save on July 31. Updates report the same number the same way, every edition, so the line can be followed without re-reading the setup.

Checkpoints and lines

Each storyline names the dates that move it, a line it rises on and a line it fades on. The status in the tracker is one of four words: Open, Rising, Fading, Closed. Closed storylines stay on the board with their ending printed.

An owner on the panel

One name per storyline. The owner writes the update and is held to the opening line printed here. The panel’s usual rules apply: they are fictional, they give opinions on what happens in the save, and they do not invent quotes for real players.

No additions

Sixteen is the list. If something bigger happens, it goes in the edition like any other story, not on the board. The desk is aware this rule will hurt by October and is keeping it anyway.

What the save gives us

Results, scorers, minutes, ratings and ability for the Academy and the graduates; results and scorers for national teams, but not minutes or ratings. A storyline’s measure is always something the save keeps.

Closing

Twelve storylines close on or before June 1, 2024, and two more on draft night in June. Two do not close this season: Kearney’s count of the national team runs to the 2026 World Cup, and the Academy’s development question runs until Garrett or Johnson reaches 170, or clearly will not.

1
Club · Georgia

The defence of everything

Can the treble winners keep any of it without the three players who made it?

Georgia won everything a club in this league can win and then lost the spine of it in one evening in Seattle. Juan Pablo Torres was the Player of the Year, Ronaldo Damus shared the scoring record and Matías Pellegrini went seventh in the draft. The model still makes them favourites, because what is left is still the best defence in the league: Sergiño Dest at 151, the joint second-highest rating in the Sixteen, Kobi Henry back from the Under-20 World Cup with a medal, and Tomás Romero, who shared the shutout record with nineteen.

Thirty-four percent is the biggest number in the field and still a minority. The question this desk will ask every month is simple: where do the goals come from? Richard Ledezma, who was player of the match fourteen times, is the obvious answer; Dest scoring the winner against Florida in pre-season is the other. The two are, respectively, in the last year of their eligibility and at the top of the 2024 board.

TB
Theo Brandt · The Ledger“Georgia were a 96-point team that scored 169 in the league. The three who left scored 94 in all competitions and set up 91 more, and the arithmetic gets uncomfortable. I will publish the goals-per-game figure every edition and we will see whether the model’s 34% was generous or stingy. My own guess is stingy on the defence and generous on the attack, which is how you get 34%.”

2
Club · Michigan

Judge us on the goalkeeper

Michigan have the deepest squad in the league for the second summer running. Is a 99-rated goalkeeper the reason they have not won it?

Two seasons, two different Michigans. In the first they finished seventh and won the Youth League, and the head coach told everybody to judge them on the 15th of May. In the second they finished second by three points, won the Rose Bowl, and conceded seventy, which is twenty-two more than the champions. The desk has had them first in its power rankings both summers. They have the best bench, the highest ceiling, Balogun with 44 goals and Miljevic with 40 assists, and the weakest first-choice goalkeeper of any contender.

Carrera has gone from 91 to 99 in a year, which is real progress and still the lowest number in the top six by a distance. Michigan scored six at Alabama in pre-season and conceded two. That is the whole storyline in one scoreline.

DR
Dale Rourke · Rourke Unfiltered“I said in June: whoever finds a goalkeeper first. Michigan are the most talented team in America and I have called them the most overrated team in America, and I will keep doing both until one of those sentences stops being true. The number I am watching is seventy. Concede fewer than fifty and they win it. Concede seventy and the desk ranks them first again next summer and I will be back here saying the same thing.”

3
Club · Texas Tech

Now, with this squad

The best starting eleven in the league is also the oldest. Nine of them leave next June. Is this the year?

Texas Tech lost two players in the draft, the fewest of anyone, and sent only Martin Robinson and Aedan Stanley to the pros. Everybody else came back, which is why the eleven rates higher than Georgia’s or Michigan’s on paper, and why Reyna, at 151, played in the Gold Cup. It is also why the clock is loud: Alexis Méndez, Owen Otasowie, Taylor Booth and six others are in the class of 2024. Whatever this group is going to do, it has one season to do it.

The desk picked them as its Climber in June. They beat Miami 2–0 and Florida State 5–3 in pre-season. The first real test is September 23 at Texas, with the Caprock Lamp on the table.

If Texas Tech keep clean sheets in September, I will believe the eleven. If they are 3–3 draws again, I will not need the board to tell me why.”

4
Club · Miami

Forty-three percent of the goals

The model has Miami third. On ability their eleven is eleventh. Which one is wrong?

Miami sent seven players to the draft, the first pick and the last pick among them, and with them went forty-three percent of last season’s goals, from a team that scored 184 in the league alone, and the goalkeeper who kept nineteen clean sheets. The replacement in goal is Eli Bradford, eighteen, rated 112, an Under-20 world champion as the second goalkeeper in Indonesia. Jordan Adebayo-Smith scored 43 and is still here, and so are the two men who set up 66 goals between them, Jonathan Perez and Miguel Ramírez.

Pre-season was the argument in miniature: 1–2 to Florida State, 0–2 to Texas Tech, then 6–2 at Texas. The model’s third place is built on last season’s results shrunk towards the mean; the eleventh-best eleven is built on today’s numbers. One of them is going to look foolish by Christmas, and the desk has a bet on which.

5
Club · Alabama

Top five is the job

The head coach’s own standard was met last season, by three points. Seven players left. Does it hold?

Two years ago the Alabama head coach said top five was the job, finished seventh, and said he had not done it. Last season Alabama finished fifth, three points clear of Oklahoma, and the sentence was satisfied by the narrowest margin it allows. The same coach spent his summer with the senior national team, winning the Nations League in March and the Gold Cup on July 16, and will go back to Tuscaloosa to find seven players gone, including the striker who scored 39.

What is left is a goalkeeper with the second-highest ceiling of any American keeper in the Academy, Gabriel Słonina, three Under-20 world champions, and José Manuel Sánchez, sixteen, with a potential of 167. The desk has them eighth. Kearney, who has been grading this coach’s sentence since it was first said, will keep the line.

Seven players out, including the goalscorer, and the man in charge spent July winning a trophy with somebody else’s players. I like him for it. I still think the board’s number is the only one that matters, and the board’s number is five.”

League · The bottom of the table

Three newcomers and a trapdoor

Two clubs go down. Rice and Tulane have never been up. TCU had eight players a month ago.

The Sixteen do not go down. The other six do, two at a time, and last season Oregon went down with four points and 290 goals conceded. Tulane and Rice come up to replace them with squads that were being built in the last week of July, and TCU, who survived in eighteenth, had eight players on the books when the desk looked on June 30 and seventeen now.

Records · The record book

Fifty-eight

Both holders of the goals record left in the draft. Who gets closest, and does anything else fall?

The record book lost its two biggest names on the same night. Damus and Iloski share the goals record at 58; one is at Colorado, the other at LAFC. Player · Daniel Garrett

129 of 200

The league’s only Diamond card is fifteen, a world champion, and about to play his first full season. How fast does the number move?

He arrived in March as a fiction this desk had built in the open, became a real player in the save, and was an Under-20 world champion by June. The other measure is minutes. He played 781 last season. The June preview said he should get three times that, which means a wing-back place in a Michigan side that still has Paredes and Che.

Player · Tyler Whitesides

Ohio State’s goalkeeper is sixteen

The two goalkeepers who played for Ohio State last season were both drafted. The first choice now is sixteen, rated 75, with the highest ceiling of any American keeper in the Academy.

The best goalkeeper at every other school in the Sixteen is rated between 91 and 123. Ohio State’s is rated 75, because Marcucci went to Pittsburgh and Ejimadu went to Houston on the same night, and the club made no signings. Tyler Whitesides is sixteen, and the file says he will be very good: a ceiling of 158 is three points above Gabriel Słonina’s, and only Florida’s Brazilian, Gabriel Luis, has a higher one among the Academy’s keepers. The file does not say when.

Ohio State conceded fourteen in three pre-season games. The desk picked them to fall from tenth to fifteenth in June and nothing in July argued with it. This storyline is the single most exposed position in the league, and Okafor, a former centre-back, will treat it as a defensive problem rather than a goalkeeper’s.

Draft · Dest and the 2024 board

Who goes first

Sixty-one players are in their last season. The board has Dest first by a distance. Does it hold, and who climbs?

Sergiño Dest is rated 151, started the Gold Cup opener, and is the only player on the 2024 board above 140. Behind him the class is Texas Tech’s: Booth, Otasowie and Méndez are three of the top six, with Brenden Aaronson of USC and James Sands of Miami between them. Aaronson’s ceiling, 145, is the highest after Dest’s. The lottery seed is sealed on December 31, as it was last year, and the order is decided in May.

Brannigan will publish the top ten every edition. Last year the top of the board barely moved between October and June; the interesting movement was always between tenth and thirtieth, which is where Pomykal fell and Matthew Real rose.

Academy · The world champions, back at school

The count

Twenty-one players won the Under-20 World Cup and all of them came back. Does the Academy make them better, or just keep them?

The wrap-up on June 17 asked the question straight and answered it halfway: the Academy had proved it could gather a generation good enough to win a world title, and had not proved it made them grow faster than they would have anyway. The twenty who had been in the league since August 2021 had gained 248 points of ability against an expected 253. That is the league rate. The desk said it would keep the count, and this is the count.

Ten weeks on, the 21 are up 21 points between them and nobody has dropped. Okoh is 145, Pepi is 146 with nine Gold Cup goals, and the two newgens at the top of the Academy’s ceilings are both up three. Brandt will print the number against expectation every edition, and the edition in June will say whether a world title changed anything about how fast these players improve.

No school lost more to the draft: eight players and 35% of last season’s minutes. Musah (146, 37 goals) and Sanogo (37 goals) are still there, and Notre Dame scored eleven in three pre-season games. The desk has them tenth.
