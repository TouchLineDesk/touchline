Special edition · Draft 23 · Seattle, Tuesday, June 20, 2023

Eighty-four names.

Seattle won the lottery and took the best player in the class at home. After that, twenty-one clubs spent four rounds disagreeing with our board, more than they did in Dallas last year, and that is what makes a draft worth grading. Every pick graded, every club graded, the steals, the reaches, the surprises, and the young man who went last.

By the Touchline desk · picks executed in the save on June 20, 2023 · grades are Touchline’s

How the night ran. The order came from the envelope Touchline sealed on December 31 and opened on lottery night: round one in lottery order, then a snake, four picks a club. Every one of the 84 graduates of the Class of 2023 was moved to his new club in the game, and the shortlist was checked top to bottom afterwards. They all landed where they were picked.

How the grades work. Three marks, all ours. The Grad Grade (A+ to D) is Brannigan’s scale for the player himself: ability, his season, make-up and headroom, with red flags docked. The pick grade is about the choice: where the player went against where our final board had him, with early reaches costing more than late ones. The club grade uses last year’s method: value against the board first, then the best player in the haul, then upside (potential minus ability across the four), then fit. Lottery position earns nobody anything.

  • Pomykal at 16. Ninth on the board, the best average rating in round one, and the seven clubs picking after his board slot passed on him.

  • Weah over Dike. The board’s No. 2 went fourth. Ceilings beat goals at the top of the draft.
  • No school sent more than eight. Notre Dame did, and only one of them went in round one.

How the mock held up

The May 30 mock, in the real order, against the real night

Our mock got 6 picks exactly right, put 9 players with the right club and had 29 of the 84 within three slots. The top of it held: Jonathan David first, and the first seven picks were the mock’s first seven players in a slightly different order. It fell apart at ten, where Pittsburgh took a player the mock had going 21st.

Exact hits: Jonathan David (1), Matías Pellegrini (7), Brady Scott (12), Diego Palacios (15), Matt Nocita (22), Rio Hope-Gund (36).

The mock assumed every club takes the best player left with a nudge away from doubling up on a role. On the night, clubs did that for the first handful of picks and then started drafting like clubs, with needs, hunches and a taste for ceilings. The board is a better guide to who is good than to who goes where, which is what a board is for.

Pick 84

The last name called in Seattle

The last pick of Draft 23 belonged to Seattle, who also had the first. They used it on Glenn Richards, a Miami walk-on who arrived this season, made two appearances with one start, played 90 minutes and made one assist.

He is rated 62, the lowest ability in the class by 27 points. His potential is 129. That gap, 67 points, is the largest in the draft by a distance; the next largest is Rob Cruz’s 41, and Seattle took him too, at 43. The club that picked first, picked last, and spent two of its four picks on the two biggest ceiling-to-ability gaps in the class.

The file also says this: his pressure rating is 3 out of 20 and his controversy rating is 15. Jess Ocampo found him first.
